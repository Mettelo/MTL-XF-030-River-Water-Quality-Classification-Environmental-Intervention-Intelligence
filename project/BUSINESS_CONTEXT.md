# MTL-XF-030 — Business context

## Organisation and pressure

**River Water Quality Classification & Environmental Intervention Intelligence.** An environmental authority needs a governed view of where ecological/chemical classifications are weakest, what pressures or measures are recorded, and which water bodies deserve further intervention investigation. This avoids dependence on a huge 72-million-observation raw archive.

The simulated sponsor, Confidential Catchment Environment & River Improvement Authority, must decide where to direct limited analytical and service-review attention. Its starting problem is an inconsistent view of river water quality classification & environmental intervention intelligence: totals, rates, periods and categories can lead users to different conclusions without a common definition. The engagement supplies an evidence base; it does not assume the client has already suffered a quantified failure.

## Current process and its consequences

Analysts retrieve official tables independently, prepare local summaries and discuss headline differences in management meetings. Without a shared cohort and definition register, revisions, incomparable denominators and missing coverage can pass into recommendations. The consequence is an investigation shortlist that cannot be explained or reproduced, rather than a verified estimate of financial loss.

## Required decision process

1. Confirm the reporting cohort and available dimensions.
2. Review governed metrics and quality exceptions before interpreting patterns.
3. Compare like-for-like trends and segment evidence against the questions below.
4. Challenge at least two prioritisation scenarios and document changes in shortlisted cases.
5. Assign a responsible role, evidence-gathering action and review point to every proposed investigation.

**Decision:** Which English river water bodies warrant further environmental investigation based on ecological/chemical classifications and recorded measures, with cycle comparability and evidence gaps made explicit?

## Business questions and evidence

| Question | Expected evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Which river water bodies have the weakest ecological or chemical status in a comparable assessment cycle? | Reconciled trend/segment table and diagnostic chart with period, grain, denominator and source references. |
| Which apparent changes may reflect methodology rather than environmental change? | Reconciled trend/segment table and diagnostic chart with period, grain, denominator and source references. |
| Where are recorded measures linked to weaker classifications? | Segment or coverage assessment stating which comparisons are supported. |
| Which bodies have missing classification or measure evidence? | Sensitivity or scope audit showing what changes the interpretation. |
| Which cases require field sampling, pressure assessment or delivery-status investigation? | Investigation plan with missing evidence, owner and decision-review trigger. |

## Intended management use

- Create a river-only classification baseline by compatible cycle, keeping ecological and chemical outcomes separate.
- Assess change only where comparability is evidenced; otherwise deliver a single-cycle baseline and a documented comparability rejection.
- Shortlist up to ten water bodies for investigation using classification weakness, missingness and recorded-measure context; test at least two classification/missingness priority rules.

The BI executive view starts with the commissioned question, reporting period and coverage. Users inspect a diagnostic view before reading the investigation shortlist. Every case records the signal, its magnitude, uncertainty, alternative explanations, evidence still needed and a next-step owner. The final briefing must explain at least one case where the evidence is insufficient for action.

## Measures of project value

Success means a repeatable review with five supported findings, five reconciled dashboard measures, two compared decision scenarios, ten UAT scenarios and a reproducible handover. Teams may propose a future operational baseline and improvement target, but must label these as proposed measures rather than achieved benefits. No observed savings or service improvement can be claimed from this simulated engagement alone.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

- Measures recorded in an administrative dataset do not prove implementation or ecological effectiveness.
- Do not average ordinal status labels into a continuous pollution score or combine chemical and ecological scales.
- Classification changes can reflect monitoring and method changes; these files do not supply raw chemical sampling histories.

## Dependencies and risks

- Source/schema validation may narrow the scope: record an explicit impact decision rather than inventing fields.
- Data revisions may alter findings: preserve immutable source manifests and rerun comparisons.
- Small, suppressed or non-comparable segments may distort ordering: apply eligibility rules and show excluded cases.
- Local context or operational records may be missing: attach an evidence request to the relevant recommendation.
